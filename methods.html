📐 Methods and limits

Scoring method and limits

How Speculum compares analog sites without inventing missing measurements, how confidence affects weights, and where Earth analogs fall short of the Moon and Mars.

1. Scoring method

No place on Earth matches the absolute climate of the Moon or Mars: Earth is warmer, wetter, and wrapped in a dense atmosphere. Measuring raw distance to Martian or lunar numbers would make almost every Earth site look equally far away. Instead, Speculum ranks the Earth sites against each other in the direction that moves closer to your chosen target.

Step-by-step calculation

  1. Choose the comparison pool: By default, the pool is the 23 documented sites. If you check Include climate-only sites on the calculator, all 89 sites enter the pool. Percentile ranks are always computed across this pool before logistics filters hide any sites.
  2. Rank each active parameter in the target's direction: For every parameter used by the target that has site data, take all typical numeric values in the pool (count = n) and compute each site's percentile rank between 0 and 1:
    rank = (number of smaller values + (number of equal values - 1) / 2) / (n - 1)
    If the target favors a higher value (such as daily temperature swing or UV radiation), similarity is sim = rank. If the target favors a lower value (such as temperature, pressure, or precipitation), similarity is sim = 1 - rank.
  3. Multiply by mission weight and confidence: Each parameter gets a weight w from 0 to 5 (prefilled from the profile you chose, or 1 if the profile does not list that parameter). Parameters with weight 0 or without site data are skipped. Each measured value also has a confidence multiplier m (1.0 for high, 0.8 for medium, 0.5 for low).
  4. Compute score and coverage:
    Score = 100 * sum(w * m * sim) / sum(w * m) (over parameters the site has)
    Coverage = 100 * sum(w of parameters the site has) / sum(w of all target parameters)
    A low score does not mean a site is useless; a low coverage percentage warns that key parameters lacked measurements at that site.
  5. Filter and sort into two tiers: Logistics filters (support station, protected area, access problems) hide ineligible sites after scoring. Remaining sites are grouped into Tier A (Documented analog of this target) first, then Tier B (Similar climate, no documented analog), each sorted from highest score to lowest.

2. Confidence rules

Every site parameter carries a confidence rating so coarse grid estimates do not outweigh direct site measurements. The multiplier reduces how strongly a low-certainty row affects the weighted average. If more than half of the scored rows at a site have low confidence, Speculum shows a warning on the result card.

High confidence 1.0× multiplier

Direct ground measurement, site station record, or published field survey for that exact location.

Medium confidence 0.8× multiplier

NASA POWER 25-year climatology (2001–2025) where the grid cell elevation and coastal setting match the field site reasonably well.

Low confidence 0.5× multiplier

Coarse grid cell in steep mountains, islands, polar ice sheets, or sites where grid elevation differs noticeably from the actual site.

4. Known limitations and next steps

Every terrestrial analog campaign must account for both universal spaceflight physics and current dataset gaps:

Dimension Status What Speculum covers

Dataset gaps and next steps

  • Slope, soil strength, radiation, and logistics data are missing: Parameters such as typical terrain slope, soil bearing strength (trafficability), surface rockiness, regolith type, ambient radiation dose, wind/dust storms, ice/permafrost, accessibility, infrastructure, permits, connectivity, and relative cost are modeled in the schema (in_data: false) but do not yet have site-by-site values.
  • Coordinates are approximate: Site latitude and longitude points represent regional centers or representative desert/crater coordinates rather than surveyed rover test tracks or habitat pads.
  • Grid data is weak in mountains and on islands: NASA POWER uses coarse reanalysis grid cells (roughly 0.5° × 0.625°), which blend sea and land on small volcanic islands (such as Tenerife, Lanzarote, Hawaii, or Jeju) and average elevation across valleys and summits in mountain ranges (such as Chajnantor or Mauna Kea).
  • Next steps: Integrate peer-reviewed slope, regolith, and ground meteorological station measurements; verify site-level permit and connectivity fields; and add primary literature citations for the climate-only candidate sites.
