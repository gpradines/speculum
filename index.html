The challenge

Before launching rovers, drills, habitats, or power hardware to permanent lunar bases or Mars, engineering teams need terrestrial field sites that stress equipment under realistic temperatures, aridity, and terrain. Finding the right testbed is difficult because published analog studies are scattered across decades of papers, and no single Earth location replicates every physical condition of another world.

What this tool can and cannot tell you

What it tells you
  • Which terrestrial sites rank closest to each other along the climate directions relevant to a Moon, Mars, or Europa target.
  • Climate baselines sourced from 25-year NASA POWER grid data (2001–2025) and published literature, each tagged with explicit high, medium, or low confidence levels.
  • Whether a site has published analog literature for your target, missing-data coverage warnings, or logistics and permit constraints.
What it cannot tell you
  • Analogs never reproduce gravity, ionizing radiation, or space vacuum: Earth testing always runs at 1 g under a thick protective atmosphere.
  • Scores are relative, not absolute: a high score ranks a site favorably against the other sites in the pool and does not mean the site physically matches lunar or Martian conditions.
  • Missing field metrics such as slope, soil bearing strength, and verified site-level logistics still require local survey checks before deployment.

Data and methods

Every number on Speculum comes from NASA POWER 25-year climatologies (2001–2025), the NASA NSSDC Moon and Mars Fact Sheets, and published analog studies. Read how the scoring works with a worked example, browse all 29 parameters, check confidence rules, and view the full source list.

Global scan

We scanned 61 regions of the world with the same climate method to look for candidates outside the known catalog. They are hypotheses based on climate only.
